The Brand

Tekion is a cloud-based platform that helps automotive dealerships manage everything, from sales and service to operations, in one unified system. It replaces outdated, clunky dealership software with modern, fast, and intuitive tools built for today’s digital world.

Tekion powers 3,000+ dealerships and is trusted by multiple OEMs. Over 440,000 vehicles have been sold through the platform.

Why this Revamp?

Tekion recognised a significant market opportunity to strengthen its value proposition by addressing a critical disconnect:

While dealerships rely on Tekion for core operations like CRM, DMS, and parts management, customer support still runs on fragmented third-party tools, leading to broken workflows, inconsistent experiences and additional training.

Defining the challenge

With everyone in the company having a different version of this “solve everything revamp” project, aligning our goals was my first challenge.

I teamed up with Aiswarya and Shaan, fellow PMs, to run a design kickoff workshop. We began with a thorough discussion of the project’s objectives, and aimed to answer two key questions:

1.How might we simplify the customer support process, reducing the current resolution time?

2.How can we set Tekion’s customer portal apart in a crowded market, and establish ourselves?

User Personas

With a baseline understanding of the requirements, research surfaced two primary personas and their pain points.

  • Had to undergo separate training to use third-party tools that aren't integrated with Tekion, leading to extra time and learning effort.
  • In case of bugs or issues, has to manually switch between platforms, increasing the chance of errors and slowing down response times.
  • Struggles with clunky, non-intuitive interfaces that aren't optimised for the dealership context.
  • Moves between the showroom, service floor and delivery areas. Third-party tools that aren’t built for mobile or tablet disrupt this on-the-go workflow and delay real-time responses.
  • Third-party tools may not show complete interaction history, previous service records, or deal context.
User Personas

The second persona represents Tekion’s new target group.

  • Often lack access to full customer history, past service interactions, or vehicle details, forcing them to piece information together manually.
  • Frequently switch between the support tool, dealership systems (like DMS/CRM), and internal portals.
  • Struggle with clunky, non-intuitive interfaces that aren't optimised for the dealership context.
  • Support tools lack real-time collaboration like tagging technicians or looping in sales/service advisors, forcing agents onto email or Slack.
  • Tickets get misrouted or poorly prioritised, leading to longer resolution times and frustrated dealer users.

Picking up the Pieces

In just four years since 2018, the internal tool grew from a minimal support portal into a slew of disparate features that made the experience slow and complex to use.

I realised it would struggle to scale alongside the company’s hyper-growth. Fundamental usability was challenged, features and experiments competed for focus, and reliability and performance issues were alarming.

Ideation & Documentation · Skill-based routing

Skill-based ticket routing sends each ticket to the agent best qualified to answer it. It matches the skills a ticket needs with those assigned to agents, so a new ticket goes to the first agent who has all the required skills and can take the work.

Pain points

Keeping track of real-time updates and making sure routing logic adapts to changing conditions.

Routing tickets to agents with the right skills, across multiple brands and a frequently changing agent list.

Misrouted tickets get rerouted again and again: slower responses, more workload, lower dealer satisfaction.

Loosely defined skills send tickets to agents who aren’t familiar with them.

Language preferences and regional requirements add another layer of complexity.

As volume grows, keeping routing efficient without degrading performance gets harder.

Critical issues can be missed at high volume, hurting customer satisfaction.

What did we change?

Follow one ticket through the revamped portal, then the admin side.

Agent · 1 of 7

Ticket created

Our dealer John can’t turn off the brand filter. As we tackle this issue, let’s look at the revamped portal.

Agent · 2 of 7

Meet your SLAs

SLAs help agents prioritise. Admins customise them by ticket type and priority. Here, we have 2 hours to get back to John.

Agent · 3 of 7

Past interactions

John’s history sits beside the ticket, new and solved, so agents never open another tab to find context.

Agent · 4 of 7

Connecting the dots

The bug already exists in Jira. Agents search and link the issue without leaving the ticket, and the status moves to Pending with Engineering.

Agent · 5 of 7

Side conversations

Message engineering on Slack, Teams or mail from inside the ticket. Collaboration no longer means switching tools.

Agent · 6 of 7

Rapid response

Engineering replies in the Slack thread inside the ticket: the filter is off for John, and a permanent fix ships next week. The agent never leaves the portal.

Agent · 7 of 7

Ticket closed

The agent updates John, he confirms it works, and the ticket closes as Resolved, all from one screen and well inside the 2-hour SLA.

Admin · 1 of 3

One hub for all configurations

A hub-and-spoke model: every setting branches from one central page, so admins always have a single place to come back to.

Admin · 2 of 3

Discoverability

Every widget carries a plain description, so admins understand its purpose before they open it.

Admin · 3 of 3

Personalised experience

Each brand switches features on or off, from thread comments and escalation to SLAs, Jira and the CRM plugin, to fit how that customer works.

Visibility for managers

Live and AI dashboards answer the finding that managers couldn’t see performance.

What I could’ve done differently

Use real support data of worst-case scenarios like long customer names, multi-line issue descriptions and large ticket volumes to stress-test layouts.

Run contextual inquiries or shadow support agents to observe how they handle tickets in real time. It reveals hidden pain points and workarounds.
